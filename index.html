Rutherford's gold foil experiment

This experiment was directed by Ernest Rutherford and performed in 1909 by Hans Geiger and Ernest Marsden at the University of Manchester.

Before that, people believed in Thomson's plum pudding model, which pictured the atom as a uniform sea of positive charge with tiny, negatively charged electrons embedded throughout it.

They used alpha particles and directed them towards an extremely thin gold foil.

If Thomson's model was true, almost all particles should pass through with only slight bending.

But the shocking truth was that:

  • Most particles went straight through without bending.
  • Some bent at noticeable angles.
  • About 1 in 8000 bounced almost straight back.

Shocked by the results, they concluded that:

  • The atom is almost all empty space.
  • The positive charge is packed in a tiny but still powerful nucleus.
  • Negative particles surround the nucleus.
